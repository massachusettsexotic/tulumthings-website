Bergen cruise port guide

Norway · from NorwayThings, the offline iPhone guide

Where ships dock

Skolten / Bontelabo (Jekteviken & Dokken for some ships)

Skolten and Bontelabo are a flat ten-minute walk from Bryggen. Ships at Jekteviken or Dokken get a free shuttle to the centre in season, or 25 minutes on foot.

Typically 7 AM – 5 PM · check the daily programme

All-aboard is in ship time. Check the daily programme and be back at the gangway 30 minutes early.

Top places near the pier

Walking times come from the app: straight-line distance plus 30% for streets, at an easy 4.5 km/h. Places further than an hour on foot show the distance instead.

  1. Bergenhus Fortress

    4 min walk · Landmarks & Stave Churches

    The fortress at the harbour mouth, between the cruise pier and Bryggen: Håkon's Hall (1261, the royal banqueting hall), the Rosenkrantz Tower with its dungeon and roof view, and lawns where the town picnics.

  2. St. Mary's Church

    10 min walk · Landmarks & Stave Churches

    Mariakirken, the oldest building in Bergen (1130s–1180s): a Romanesque stone church with twin towers, a gilded Baroque pulpit and a 15th-century altarpiece, once the German merchants' church.

  3. Bergen Aquarium

    10 min walk · Family Fun

    Akvariet on the Nordnes peninsula: penguins, sea lions, seals, crocodiles and the North Sea tanks, with feeding shows through the day.

  4. Bryggens Museum

    10 min walk · Museums & Culture

    Built over the excavated foundations of the medieval town: the oldest houses in Bergen under glass, runic sticks with 800-year-old love notes and complaints, and the story of how the Hanseatic merchants ran the place.

  5. Bryggen

    11 min walk · Landmarks & Stave Churches

    The Hanseatic wharf: a row of leaning wooden warehouses from the 1700s on medieval foundations, a UNESCO World Heritage Site and the picture of Bergen.

  6. Nordnes Sjøbad

    11 min walk · Beaches & Fjord Swims

    A heated outdoor seawater pool on the tip of Nordnes, with a diving board straight into the fjord and a sauna: Bergen's summer swimming spot, a ten-minute walk from the cruise pier.

  7. Bryggeloftet & Stuene

    13 min walk · Restaurants

    The traditional restaurant on Bryggen since 1910: fish soup, klippfisk, reindeer and whale on the menu, wooden booths and paintings of the harbour.

  8. Rødne Fjord Cruise Bergen

    14 min walk · Fjord Cruises & Boat Trips

    The three-hour fjord cruise from the fish market to Mostraumen — a narrow sound with waterfalls close enough to fill a glass, seals and a cliff-hugging farm.

  9. Fisketorget & Mathallen

    15 min walk · Fish Markets & Farm Shops

    The fish market on the harbour since the 1200s: outdoor stalls of salmon, king crab and shrimp in summer, and the glass Mathallen hall year-round with fishmongers, a few restaurants and the tourist information upstairs.

  10. Fløibanen Funicular

    18 min walk · Hikes, Glaciers & Rides

    The funicular from the middle of town to the top of Mount Fløyen, 320 m up, in six minutes: the view over Bergen, the fjord and the islands, a café, goats, a playground and the start of a dozen hikes.

  11. Galleriet

    18 min walk · Shopping & Souvenirs

    The shopping centre on Torgallmenningen, the main square: 70 shops including Norwegian knitwear (Dale of Norway), outdoor gear and a supermarket in the basement.

  12. Kaffemisjonen

    18 min walk · Cafés & Bakeries

    Bergen's coffee-roaster café on Øvre Korskirkeallmenningen: proper espresso, cinnamon buns (skillingsboller — the local speciality), and a window on the old town.

  13. Pingvinen

    19 min walk · Restaurants

    Norwegian home cooking in a pub off the main square: meatballs, fish cakes, raspeballer dumplings on Thursdays, lamb stew in autumn, and local beer.

  14. KODE Art Museums

    25 min walk · Museums & Culture

    Four museum buildings along the lake in the centre: Munch's Bergen collection and Norwegian romantics in KODE 3 (Rasmus Meyer), international art in KODE 2, design and silver in KODE 1, and Astrup's fjord paintings.

  15. Leprosy Museum

    25 min walk · Museums & Culture

    St. Jørgen's Hospital, a leprosy hospital from the 1700s left exactly as it was when the last patients died in 1946: tiny wooden cells, the chapel, and the story of Armauer Hansen, who discovered the leprosy bacillus here.

Getting around: what the app tells you

Ship time, tender time, and the last boat back
All-aboard is in ship's time, which is not always local time — check the daily programme. In Geiranger the last tender back runs 30–60 minutes before all-aboard and the queue is longest right then; in Flåm the train and the fjord boat both run late on two-ship days. Be at the gangway 30 minutes early. The ship waits for its own excursions, not for you.
Book the operators direct, not the ship's desk
The Flåm Railway, Nærøyfjord boat, Loen Skylift, Fløibanen, Lysefjord and Geiranger cruises all sell online at fixed prices; the ship's excursion desk sells the same seats with a mark-up and a bus you didn't need. Book direct weeks ahead for anything with limited seats, and check the port's arrival time against the departure you pick — with an hour's margin.
Check the webcam before you pay for the view
Dalsnibba, the Loen Skylift, Fløyen, Ulriken and Stegastein all have live webcams; the mountain can be in cloud while the pier is sunny. Look before you buy the bus or the lift, and if the top is grey, do the fjord boat or the museum first and go up later — weather in the fjords changes in an hour.
