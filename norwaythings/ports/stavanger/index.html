Stavanger cruise port guide

Norway · from NorwayThings, the offline iPhone guide

Where ships dock

Vågen — Strandkaien & Skagenkaien

You step off into the old harbour; Gamle Stavanger, the cathedral and the Lysefjord boats are all within five minutes.

Typically 8 AM – 5 PM

All-aboard is in ship time. Check the daily programme and be back at the gangway 30 minutes early.

Top places near the pier

Walking times come from the app: straight-line distance plus 30% for streets, at an easy 4.5 km/h. Places further than an hour on foot show the distance instead.

  1. Bøker og Børst

    1 min walk · Bars & Nightlife

    The bar in Fargegaten lined with books and mismatched furniture, with a courtyard, local beer, and Stavanger's students and oil engineers side by side.

  2. Øvre Holmegate

    1 min walk · Landmarks & Stave Churches

    Fargegaten, the colour street: a lane of cafés, bars and shops painted in a hairdresser's 2005 colour scheme — pink, turquoise, lime — that turned a dead street into the town's living room.

  3. Norwegian Petroleum Museum

    2 min walk · Museums & Culture

    How a fishing town became the oil capital: real drill bits, a lifeboat you can sit in, a full-size diving bell, the story of the Alexander Kielland disaster, and the ethics of it all, in a building shaped like a rig on the harbour.

  4. Valbergtårnet

    3 min walk · Landmarks & Stave Churches

    The 1853 fire watchtower on the hill between the harbour and the cathedral, with a little museum and a view over the quay and the ships.

  5. Rødne Fjord Cruise Stavanger

    4 min walk · Fjord Cruises & Boat Trips

    The three-hour Lysefjord cruise from the quay: under Preikestolen, into the Vagabond's Cave, past waterfalls and goats, with a stop at Fantahålå.

  6. Renaa Matbaren

    5 min walk · Restaurants

    The casual bistro of Stavanger's Michelin-starred chef Sven Erik Renaa: small plates, local lamb and fish, natural wine, at a fraction of the tasting-menu price.

  7. Fisketorget Stavanger

    6 min walk · Restaurants

    The fish restaurant on the quay with the fish counter downstairs and the dining room upstairs over the boats: fish soup, shellfish platters, the day's catch.

  8. Stavanger Cathedral

    6 min walk · Landmarks & Stave Churches

    Norway's oldest cathedral (1125), Anglo-Norman with a Gothic chancel, restored for its 900th anniversary and reopened in 2024 with the stone cleaned and the Baroque pulpit gleaming.

  9. Stavanger Maritime Museum

    6 min walk · Museums & Culture

    Two merchant houses on the quay, with the sailing-ship and herring-trade story of the town, a 19th-century general store and a shipowner's flat upstairs.

  10. Gamle Stavanger

    7 min walk · Landmarks & Stave Churches

    Old Stavanger: 173 whitewashed wooden houses from the 1700s and 1800s on cobbled lanes above the harbour, still lived in, with roses on the fences and the odd gallery.

  11. Norwegian Canning Museum

    8 min walk · Museums & Culture

    A sardine cannery from 1916 in the middle of Gamle Stavanger, with the smoking ovens lit on certain days: how Stavanger fed the world brisling in tins with Norwegian labels, before oil.

  12. Byparken & Breiavatnet

    8 min walk · Parks & Nature

    The park around the lake in the centre, behind the cathedral: swans, benches, the railway station on the far side and the whole town walking through.

  13. Godalen Beach

    44 min walk · Beaches & Fjord Swims

    The town beach, 2 km south of the centre on the fjord: sand, grass, a diving raft and a kiosk in summer, and locals swimming from May.

  14. Swords in Rock

    4 mi / 6 km from the pier — taxi or shuttle · Landmarks & Stave Churches

    Sverd i fjell: three ten-metre bronze swords driven into the rock at Hafrsfjord, marking the battle of 872 where Harald Fairhair united Norway.

  15. Flor & Fjære

    8 mi / 13 km from the pier — taxi or shuttle · Parks & Nature

    A tropical garden on an island twenty minutes by boat from the harbour: palms, thousands of flowers and a lunch buffet in a family's private paradise, open May to September.

Getting around: what the app tells you

Ship time, tender time, and the last boat back
All-aboard is in ship's time, which is not always local time — check the daily programme. In Geiranger the last tender back runs 30–60 minutes before all-aboard and the queue is longest right then; in Flåm the train and the fjord boat both run late on two-ship days. Be at the gangway 30 minutes early. The ship waits for its own excursions, not for you.
Book the operators direct, not the ship's desk
The Flåm Railway, Nærøyfjord boat, Loen Skylift, Fløibanen, Lysefjord and Geiranger cruises all sell online at fixed prices; the ship's excursion desk sells the same seats with a mark-up and a bus you didn't need. Book direct weeks ahead for anything with limited seats, and check the port's arrival time against the departure you pick — with an hour's margin.
Check the webcam before you pay for the view
Dalsnibba, the Loen Skylift, Fløyen, Ulriken and Stegastein all have live webcams; the mountain can be in cloud while the pier is sunny. Look before you buy the bus or the lift, and if the top is grey, do the fjord boat or the museum first and go up later — weather in the fjords changes in an hour.
