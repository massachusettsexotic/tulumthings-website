Where ships dock

Two minutes to the Rauma railway, the gondola and the Trollstigen buses. Book all three before you sail.

Typically 8 AM – 5 PM

Top places near the pier

Walking times come from the app: straight-line distance plus 30% for streets, at an easy 4.5 km/h. Places further than an hour on foot show the distance instead.

  1. Rauma Railway

    2 min walk · Hikes, Glaciers & Rides

    The 114 km line from Åndalsnes to Dombås through the Romsdal valley: the Kylling Bridge, the Troll Wall, the spiral tunnels — voted one of the most beautiful train rides in Europe.

  2. Norwegian Mountaineering Centre (Tindesenteret)

    2 min walk · Museums & Culture

    The climbing museum and visitor centre by the station: the history of Romsdal climbing, a 21-metre indoor climbing wall — Norway's tallest — and the best café in town.

  3. Romsdalsgondolen

    4 min walk · Hikes, Glaciers & Rides

    The gondola from the town centre to the top of Nesaksla (708 m) in eight minutes: the Romsdalsfjord, the Rauma valley and the Troll Wall laid out from the Eggen restaurant terrace at the top.

  4. Rampestreken Viewpoint

    27 min walk · Waterfalls & Viewpoints

    The steel platform cantilevered out over the valley 537 m above Åndalsnes, on the Romsdalseggen path — reached by the gondola and a short walk, or by 1,300 stone steps from town.

  5. Romsdalseggen Ridge

    49 min walk · Hikes, Glaciers & Rides

    The classic ridge hike above Åndalsnes: 10 km along the crest with the Troll Wall, the fjord and the valley below, 6–8 hours, bus to the start at Venjesdalen.

  6. Trollveggen (Troll Wall)

    8 mi / 13 km from the pier — taxi or shuttle · Waterfalls & Viewpoints

    Europe's tallest vertical rock face — 1,100 m of overhanging granite — on the east side of the Romsdal valley, with a visitor centre and café at its foot on the E136.

  7. Trollstigen

    10 mi / 16 km from the pier — taxi or shuttle · Waterfalls & Viewpoints

    The Troll's Ladder: eleven hairpins climbing 850 m up a cliff face, the Stigfossen waterfall crashing under the road, and a glass-and-steel viewing platform at the top.

Getting around: what the app tells you

Ship time, tender time, and the last boat back
All-aboard is in ship's time, which is not always local time — check the daily programme. In Geiranger the last tender back runs 30–60 minutes before all-aboard and the queue is longest right then; in Flåm the train and the fjord boat both run late on two-ship days. Be at the gangway 30 minutes early. The ship waits for its own excursions, not for you.
Book the operators direct, not the ship's desk
The Flåm Railway, Nærøyfjord boat, Loen Skylift, Fløibanen, Lysefjord and Geiranger cruises all sell online at fixed prices; the ship's excursion desk sells the same seats with a mark-up and a bus you didn't need. Book direct weeks ahead for anything with limited seats, and check the port's arrival time against the departure you pick — with an hour's margin.
Check the webcam before you pay for the view
Dalsnibba, the Loen Skylift, Fløyen, Ulriken and Stegastein all have live webcams; the mountain can be in cloud while the pier is sunny. Look before you buy the bus or the lift, and if the top is grey, do the fjord boat or the museum first and go up later — weather in the fjords changes in an hour.
