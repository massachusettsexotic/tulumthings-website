Oslo cruise port guide

Norway · from NorwayThings, the offline iPhone guide

Where ships dock

Søndre Akershuskai / Revierkaia / Vippetangen (Filipstad for some ships)

Under Akershus Fortress: the City Hall, the Opera and the Bygdøy museum ferry are all within ten minutes on foot. Filipstad ships get a shuttle or a 25-minute waterfront walk.

Typically 7 AM – 5 PM

All-aboard is in ship time. Check the daily programme and be back at the gangway 30 minutes early.

Top places near the pier

Walking times come from the app: straight-line distance plus 30% for streets, at an easy 4.5 km/h. Places further than an hour on foot show the distance instead.

  1. Akershus Fortress

    7 min walk · Landmarks & Stave Churches

    The medieval castle and Renaissance palace that has guarded Oslo's harbour since 1299: ramparts with the best free view of the fjord, the castle interiors, the Resistance Museum and the Armed Forces Museum inside the walls.

  2. Oslo Opera House

    14 min walk · Landmarks & Stave Churches

    Snøhetta's white marble opera house rises out of the fjord like an iceberg you can walk on: the sloping roof is a public plaza with the whole harbour laid out below.

  3. Munch Museum

    15 min walk · Museums & Culture

    Thirteen floors of Edvard Munch on the waterfront next to the Opera: three versions of The Scream (shown in rotation), the Madonna, the self-portraits, and a top-floor bar with the fjord view.

  4. Oslo City Hall

    16 min walk · Landmarks & Stave Churches

    The two brown-brick towers where the Nobel Peace Prize is handed out every December: the main hall is covered in murals of Norwegian life and history, and entry is free.

  5. Aker Brygge & Tjuvholmen

    18 min walk · Restaurants

    The old shipyard turned waterfront strip: restaurants and bars on the boardwalk, the Astrup Fearnley modern art museum by Renzo Piano at the tip of Tjuvholmen, and a small harbour beach.

  6. National Museum

    18 min walk · Museums & Culture

    Norway's new national museum by the City Hall: the largest art museum in the Nordics, with Munch's original 1893 Scream, the national romantic landscapes, design and the Viking-age Baldishol tapestry.

  7. Mathallen Oslo

    37 min walk · Fish Markets & Farm Shops

    The food hall in a converted factory on the Akerselva river: thirty stalls and counters — fish, cheese, Norwegian charcuterie, bakeries, a wine bar — under one roof.

  8. Fram Museum

    39 min walk · Museums & Culture

    The polar ship Fram — Nansen's and Amundsen's — under a tent-shaped roof on Bygdøy, and you can walk her decks.

  9. Kon-Tiki Museum

    41 min walk · Museums & Culture

    Thor Heyerdahl's balsa raft Kon-Tiki, the reed boat Ra II, and the Easter Island moai from his expeditions, in a small museum opposite the Fram.

  10. Vigeland Sculpture Park

    49 min walk · Parks & Nature

    Gustav Vigeland's 212 bronze and granite figures in Frogner Park — the Monolith of 121 intertwined bodies, the Angry Boy, the wheel of life — in the most-visited park in Norway.

  11. Norwegian Folk Museum

    52 min walk · Museums & Culture

    Europe's oldest open-air museum: 160 historic buildings moved to Bygdøy, including the 1200 Gol stave church, a Sami camp and an Oslo apartment block furnished decade by decade.

  12. Viking Ship Museum (closed for rebuilding)

    54 min walk · Museums & Culture

    The three best-preserved Viking ships in the world — Oseberg, Gokstad and Tune — are being rehoused in the new Museum of the Viking Age on Bygdøy, scheduled to reopen in 2027.

  13. Holmenkollen Ski Jump

    6 mi / 10 km from the pier — taxi or shuttle · Hikes, Glaciers & Rides

    The ski jump on the hill above Oslo — the world's oldest, rebuilt in steel in 2010 — with a ski museum inside the hill and a viewing platform on top of the tower that takes in the whole fjord.

Getting around: what the app tells you

Ship time, tender time, and the last boat back
All-aboard is in ship's time, which is not always local time — check the daily programme. In Geiranger the last tender back runs 30–60 minutes before all-aboard and the queue is longest right then; in Flåm the train and the fjord boat both run late on two-ship days. Be at the gangway 30 minutes early. The ship waits for its own excursions, not for you.
Book the operators direct, not the ship's desk
The Flåm Railway, Nærøyfjord boat, Loen Skylift, Fløibanen, Lysefjord and Geiranger cruises all sell online at fixed prices; the ship's excursion desk sells the same seats with a mark-up and a bus you didn't need. Book direct weeks ahead for anything with limited seats, and check the port's arrival time against the departure you pick — with an hour's margin.
Check the webcam before you pay for the view
Dalsnibba, the Loen Skylift, Fløyen, Ulriken and Stegastein all have live webcams; the mountain can be in cloud while the pier is sunny. Look before you buy the bus or the lift, and if the top is grey, do the fjord boat or the museum first and go up later — weather in the fjords changes in an hour.
