Valletta cruise port guide

Malta · from MaltaThings, the offline iPhone guide

Where ships dock

Valletta Cruise Port (Grand Harbour waterfront)

Every ship docks below the walls. The Barrakka Lift (€1) takes you up into the city in 25 seconds; the Three Cities are a €1.50 boat ride across the harbour; every bus on the island starts at the terminus outside the City Gate.

Typically 7 AM – 6 PM · overnight calls are common in summer

All-aboard is in ship time. Check the daily programme and be back at the gangway 30 minutes early.

Top places near the pier

Walking times come from the app: straight-line distance plus 30% for streets, at an easy 4.5 km/h. Places further than an hour on foot show the distance instead.

  1. Upper Barrakka Gardens

    6 min walk · Gardens & Nature

    A colonnaded garden on the highest bastion of Valletta, with the definitive view over the Grand Harbour to the Three Cities.

  2. Lascaris War Rooms

    6 min walk · Museums & Palaces

    The tunnels under the Upper Barrakka where the defence of Malta and the invasion of Sicily were run in 1943: the RAF fighter control room, the operations rooms, the Enigma-fed plotting tables, all restored to the day they were used.

  3. Senglea Gardjola Gardens

    8 min walk · Gardens & Nature

    The little garden at the tip of Senglea with the famous watchtower carved with an eye and an ear — the Knights' sentry symbol — and the view back across the harbour to Valletta and the cruise ships.

  4. MUŻA National Museum of Art

    8 min walk · Museums & Palaces

    Malta's national art collection in the restored Auberge d'Italie, one of the Knights' inns: Mattia Preti's Baroque canvases, the Maltese painters, and a good café in the courtyard.

  5. St John's Co-Cathedral

    11 min walk · Temples, Forts & Churches

    Plain limestone outside, the most lavishly decorated church interior in Europe inside: every inch gilded by the Knights of Malta, a floor of 400 marble tombstones, and Caravaggio's largest painting, the Beheading of St John, in the Oratory.

  6. Is-Suq tal-Belt (Valletta Food Market)

    13 min walk · Markets & Food Halls

    The Victorian iron market hall on Merchants Street, rebuilt as a food hall: a dozen counters of Maltese and world food, a supermarket in the basement, and tables on three floors.

  7. Republic Street

    13 min walk · Temples, Forts & Churches

    Valletta's spine, running the length of the city from the City Gate to Fort St Elmo: shops, cafés, palazzi, the Co-Cathedral, the Palace and the National Library along a straight limestone street on a grid laid out in 1566.

  8. Caffe Cordina

    13 min walk · Cafés & Pastizzi

    Valletta's grand café since 1837, on Republic Square under the statue of Queen Victoria: pastizzi, cannoli, kannoli tal-irkotta, coffee under painted ceilings inside or at the tables in the square.

  9. Legligin

    13 min walk · Restaurants

    A wine bar in a cellar on St Lucia Street doing a nine-course Maltese tasting menu — bigilla bean dip, rabbit, snails, sausage, fish — at one price, with a Maltese wine list.

  10. Fort St Angelo

    13 min walk · Temples, Forts & Churches

    The fortress at the tip of Birgu that was the Knights' headquarters in 1565 and held the Grand Harbour through the siege — and the Royal Navy's HMS St Angelo through 1942.

  11. Grandmaster's Palace

    13 min walk · Museums & Palaces

    The palace of the Grand Masters of the Knights, later the Governor's residence and Malta's parliament: the State Rooms with the Great Siege frescoes, the tapestry chamber, and the Palace Armoury with 5,000 pieces of the Knights' armour and…

  12. Rubino

    13 min walk · Restaurants

    A Valletta restaurant since 1906 in an old confectionery on Old Bakery Street: rabbit stew, bragioli (stuffed beef rolls), whatever the boats brought in, and a cassata siciliana that people cross the island for.

  13. Strait Street

    14 min walk · Bars & Nightlife

    The Gut: the narrow street where sailors of every navy drank and fought for two centuries, dead by the 1980s and now Valletta's bar strip again, with jazz, wine bars and cocktails in the old dance halls.

  14. Manoel Theatre

    15 min walk · Museums & Palaces

    One of Europe's oldest working theatres (1731), a jewel-box of gilded boxes built by the Knights so the young ones would stay out of trouble.

  15. Lower Barrakka Gardens & Siege Bell

    15 min walk · Gardens & Nature

    The quieter sister garden at the harbour mouth, with a Doric temple, a view straight out to sea past Fort Ricasoli, and the Siege Bell Memorial next door — the great bell rung daily at noon for the 7,000 who died in the 1940–43 siege.

Getting around: what the app tells you

€1 lift up, free lift down, noon gun in between
The Barrakka Lift beside the cruise terminal is the whole secret to Valletta on a port day: 25 seconds instead of a 15-minute climb, and it lands you in the best garden in the city. Time your return through the gardens for 11:55 and you get the Saluting Battery's noon gun for free from the terrace above. The lift down is free; the queue back at the end of the day is the only wait.
Book St John's online; book the Hypogeum months ahead
St John's Co-Cathedral sells timed tickets online and the ship-day queue at the door can be an hour; the Hypogeum admits 80 people a day and sells out weeks or months ahead, with a handful of next-day tickets released at Fort St Elmo. Everything else — the Palace, the temples, the catacombs, Gozo's Cittadella — is Heritage Malta, walk-in, and cheaper with the multi-site pass if you'll see three or more. Shoulders and knees covered in every church; no heels on the cathedral floor.
Sunday is Marsaxlokk; Monday half the museums shut
If your port day is a Sunday, the fish market at Marsaxlokk is the morning, St John's is closed, and the shops are shut — plan around the harbour and the temples. Some smaller museums and the Manoel Theatre tours close Mondays; Heritage Malta's big sites open daily. Village festa season (June to September) means fireworks, brass bands and a saint carried through the streets somewhere on the island most weekends — ask the tourist office which village is celebrating.
