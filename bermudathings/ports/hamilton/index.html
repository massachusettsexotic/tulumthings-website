Hamilton cruise port guide

Bermuda · from BermudaThings, the offline iPhone guide

Where ships dock

Front Street cruise berth, Hamilton

Only smaller ships dock here, on Front Street in the middle of the city; the ferry and bus terminals are a few minutes' walk. Most ships use the Royal Naval Dockyard instead — from there, Hamilton is 20 minutes on the Blue ferry.

Many ships stay overnight or longer · Bermuda is one hour ahead of US Eastern time — check which clock your ship keeps

All-aboard is in ship time. Check the daily programme and be back at the gangway 30 minutes early.

Top places near the pier

Walking times come from the app: straight-line distance plus 30% for streets, at an easy 4.5 km/h. Places further than an hour on foot show the distance instead.

  1. Front Street

    1 min walk · Forts & Landmarks

    Hamilton's harbourfront of pastel-painted Victorian verandas — banks, jewellers, Bermuda-shorts tailors and pubs with balconies over the water.

  2. Rock Island Coffee

    2 min walk · Cafés & Bakeries

    Hamilton's coffee roaster in an old house on Reid Street: proper espresso, local art on the walls, and a veranda where the city's creative types and office escapees sit.

  3. Hog Penny

    3 min walk · Restaurants

    Hamilton's pub since 1957, a dark-panelled English tavern on Burnaby Street that inspired the bar in 'Cheers': fish chowder, bangers and mash, curries, Dark 'n' Stormys and live music.

  4. Sessions House

    3 min walk · Forts & Landmarks

    Bermuda's Parliament, sitting since 1826 in a Georgian building with a red-brick Italianate clock tower added for Queen Victoria's jubilee.

  5. The Pickled Onion

    4 min walk · Restaurants

    A Front Street balcony over the harbour — the classic Hamilton lunch or sunset drink: fish tacos, wahoo, burgers and rum swizzles, with live music most evenings.

  6. Bermuda Cathedral

    4 min walk · Forts & Landmarks

    The Cathedral of the Most Holy Trinity (1894), Hamilton's neo-Gothic landmark in Bermuda limestone with Scottish stone and Caen-stone carving: climb the 157 steps of the tower for the best view over the city, the harbour and the Great…

  7. City Hall & Arts Centre

    6 min walk · Museums & Culture

    Hamilton's 1960 City Hall, a Wil Onions building with a weathervane of the Sea Venture, houses the Bermuda National Gallery and the Bermuda Society of Arts upstairs — European old masters, African art and the island's own painters.

  8. Fort Hamilton

    10 min walk · Forts & Landmarks

    A Victorian fort on the hill east of the city, with a dry moat turned into a tropical garden, tunnels in the walls, 18-ton guns on the ramparts and a panorama of Hamilton Harbour.

  9. Bermuda Underwater Exploration Institute

    15 min walk · Museums & Culture

    BUEI, on the harbour road east of town: shipwreck treasure, a walk-in bathysphere, the Bermuda Triangle explained by scientists rather than television, a shark-cage simulator, and the Harbourfront restaurant upstairs.

  10. Art Mel's Spicy Dicy

    23 min walk · Restaurants

    The fish sandwich Bermudians argue is the best: a slab of fried fish on raisin bread the size of a paperback, with coleslaw, tartar and hot sauce, from a takeaway counter in a residential Pembroke street.

  11. Ducking Stool Park

    27 min walk · Parks & Reserves

    A small grassy headland park on North Shore Road, Pembroke, with the ducking-stool point at its tip: picnic grass, a swimming spot off the rocks, and a front-row seat for cruise ships entering and leaving Hamilton Harbour.

  12. Ducking Stool

    28 min walk · Forts & Landmarks

    A rocky point on the North Shore in Pembroke, named for the ducking stool that stood here in the 1600s: petty offenders and 'scolds' were strapped into a chair on the end of a beam and dunked in the sea while the parish watched.

  13. Bermuda Botanical Gardens

    32 min walk · Parks & Reserves

    Thirty-six acres of palms, cedars, hibiscus, a sensory garden, banyans, a moongate and the island's best lawns, a mile from Hamilton.

  14. Masterworks Museum of Bermuda Art

    34 min walk · Museums & Culture

    Bermuda as painted by visitors: Winslow Homer's watercolours, Georgia O'Keeffe's banyans, Marsden Hartley, Charles Demuth, and a strong local collection, in a purpose-built gallery inside the Botanical Gardens.

  15. Elbow Beach

    39 min walk · Beaches

    A long curve of pink sand in Paget, with a reef just offshore and the wreck of the Pollockshields (1915) in the shallows for snorkellers.

Getting around: what the app tells you

Take the ferry out, but know the last one back
The Blue ferry to Hamilton is 20 minutes and the best view on the island; the bus is 50 minutes. The catch is timing: ferries run roughly hourly, the Orange ferry to St. George's only three or four times a day, and on ship days the last sailing back to the Dockyard fills up. Photograph the timetable at the dock, aim for the sailing before the one you need, and keep a taxi fare in reserve — taxis are the only thing that never leaves without you.
Read the bill before you tip
Restaurants, pubs and hotels add a 17% (sometimes 15%) gratuity to the bill automatically, and the card machine will still ask if you'd like to add a tip. Look for 'gratuity' or 'service' on the bill; if it's there, a small round-up is generous. Menu prices are otherwise what you pay — there is no sales tax in Bermuda.
Look right, drive left, and take the bus to the beach
Traffic keeps to the left, the roads are narrow with no shoulders, and the 35 km/h limit is real. Pedestrians: look right first. On a scooter, ride like a local — no overtaking on bends, helmet done up, and don't rent one for the first time on a wet day. If in doubt, bus 7 goes from the Dockyard past every South Shore beach to Hamilton, and it's a lovely ride.
