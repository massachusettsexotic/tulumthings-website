St. George's cruise port guide

Bermuda · from BermudaThings, the offline iPhone guide

Where ships dock

Ordnance Island cruise berth, St. George's

Only small ships dock here, on Ordnance Island facing King's Square. Most ships use the Royal Naval Dockyard instead — from there, the Orange ferry (April–October, Monday–Saturday) takes 45 minutes.

Many ships stay overnight or longer · Bermuda is one hour ahead of US Eastern time — check which clock your ship keeps

All-aboard is in ship time. Check the daily programme and be back at the gangway 30 minutes early.

Top places near the pier

  1. Ordnance Island & the Deliverance

    1 min walk · Forts & Landmarks

    The little island off King's Square, reached by a bridge, where a replica of the Deliverance stands — the ship the Sea Venture survivors built from Bermuda cedar in 1610 to finish their voyage to Jamestown.

  2. White Horse Pub & Restaurant

    1 min walk · Restaurants

    A waterside pub on King's Square where you feed the fish off the deck between bites of fish and chips, curry and Bermuda fish chowder, and watch the ferry come in.

  3. Wahoo's Bistro & Patio

    2 min walk · Restaurants

    The St. George's fish restaurant on Water Street, run by a fisherman's family: wahoo chowder, fish sandwiches, grilled catch of the day and rockfish, on a patio over the harbour.

  8. Unfinished Church

    7 min walk · Forts & Landmarks

    A Gothic cathedral begun in 1874 to replace St. Peter's and abandoned when the parish fell out, the money ran out and a hurricane took the roof — leaving arches, pillars and grass under an open sky.

  9. Tobacco Bay Beach

    18 min walk · Snorkel & Dive

    A small cove of towering limestone formations fifteen minutes' walk from King's Square, calm and shallow with the East End's best snorkelling among the rocks — and a beach bar, rentals, sun beds and music in season.

  10. St. Catherine's Beach

    19 min walk · Beaches

    The wide, uncrowded beach under the walls of Fort St. Catherine, where Bermuda's history began in 1609.

  11. Alexandra Battery & Building's Bay

    20 min walk · Beaches

    A 1860s coastal battery at the eastern end of town with rusting guns and a view down Town Cut — and below it a small beach of sea glass, where a century of bottles tossed into the sea comes back polished.

  12. Achilles Bay

    20 min walk · Beaches

    A pocket of sand between Tobacco Bay and Fort St. Catherine, sheltered, clear, and usually quieter than its famous neighbour — good snorkelling off the rocks on both sides and a lifeguard in summer some years.

Getting around: what the app tells you

Take the ferry out, but know the last one back
The Blue ferry to Hamilton is 20 minutes and the best view on the island; the bus is 50 minutes. The catch is timing: ferries run roughly hourly, the Orange ferry to St. George's only three or four times a day, and on ship days the last sailing back to the Dockyard fills up. Photograph the timetable at the dock, aim for the sailing before the one you need, and keep a taxi fare in reserve — taxis are the only thing that never leaves without you.
Read the bill before you tip
Restaurants, pubs and hotels add a 17% (sometimes 15%) gratuity to the bill automatically, and the card machine will still ask if you'd like to add a tip. Look for 'gratuity' or 'service' on the bill; if it's there, a small round-up is generous. Menu prices are otherwise what you pay — there is no sales tax in Bermuda.
Look right, drive left, and take the bus to the beach
Traffic keeps to the left, the roads are narrow with no shoulders, and the 35 km/h limit is real. Pedestrians: look right first. On a scooter, ride like a local — no overtaking on bends, helmet done up, and don't rent one for the first time on a wet day. If in doubt, bus 7 goes from the Dockyard past every South Shore beach to Hamilton, and it's a lovely ride.
