Sitka cruise port guide

Alaska · from AlaskaThings, the offline iPhone guide

Where ships dock

Sitka Sound Cruise Terminal (Halibut Point)

Most ships dock 5 mi north of town at Halibut Point — free shuttle buses run every few minutes to downtown (15 min). Smaller ships tender to the downtown lightering dock instead.

Typically 7 AM – 4 PM

All-aboard is in ship time. Check the daily programme and be back at the gangway 30 minutes early.

Top places near the pier

Walking times come from the app: straight-line distance plus 30% for streets, at an easy 4.5 km/h. Places further than an hour on foot show the distance instead.

  1. Russian Bishop's House

    7 mi / 11 km from the pier — taxi or shuttle · Landmarks & History

    One of only four surviving Russian colonial buildings in North America (1842), restored by the Park Service — the bishop's chapel and quarters upstairs, exhibits below.

  2. St. Michael's Cathedral

    7 mi / 11 km from the pier — taxi or shuttle · Landmarks & History

    The onion-domed Russian Orthodox cathedral in the middle of Lincoln Street, rebuilt after a 1966 fire with its original icons saved by a human chain.

  3. Castle Hill

    7 mi / 11 km from the pier — taxi or shuttle · Landmarks & History

    The low hill where Alaska was handed from Russia to the United States in 1867 — cannons, a flagpole, and the best 360° view of Sitka Sound and Mount Edgecumbe.

  4. Sheldon Jackson Museum

    7 mi / 11 km from the pier — taxi or shuttle · Museums & Galleries

    Alaska's oldest museum (1897), an octagonal concrete building full of Native artifacts collected across the whole territory — masks, kayaks, tools, in the original glass cases.

  5. Sitka Sound Science Center

    7 mi / 11 km from the pier — taxi or shuttle · Family Fun

    A working salmon hatchery and small aquarium on the waterfront with touch tanks — free to look around, cheap to go inside.

  6. Sitka National Historical Park

    7 mi / 11 km from the pier — taxi or shuttle · Totems & Native Culture

    Totem poles along a mile of rainforest trail to the 1804 battleground where the Tlingit fought the Russians — Alaska's oldest national park, with a visitor center where carvers work.

  7. Alaska Raptor Center

    7 mi / 12 km from the pier — taxi or shuttle · Wildlife & Bears

    A bald-eagle hospital and rehabilitation center on the edge of the national park — eagles, owls and hawks recovering in flight-training enclosures, seen from indoors and out.

  8. Fortress of the Bear

    11 mi / 18 km from the pier — taxi or shuttle · Wildlife & Bears

    A rescue center for orphaned brown and black bears in a former pulp-mill clarifier 5 mi from town — you watch from a platform a few feet above them.

Getting around: what the app tells you

Book direct, not through the ship
The same whale boat, train or floatplane typically costs 30–60% less on the operator's own website than in the ship's excursion catalog. The ship's price buys one thing: the guarantee it will wait. In port towns this small, that's rarely worth the difference.
Check whose clock you're on
Ships often stay on Pacific time while Alaska runs an hour behind. Your phone switches; the ship's clocks don't. Set a manual alarm in ship time for all-aboard.
Ask for the return guarantee
Most Juneau whale-watch and glacier operators, and the White Pass railroad, promise to have you back before all-aboard or cover your way to the next port. If the operator offers it, the ship's markup buys nothing.
The jewelry strip is not local
The same chains fill the storefronts by the dock in every port and pay the cruise lines for the 'recommended store' map. Buy Native art at the heritage centers with the Silver Hand seal, and anything else a block or two back from the pier.
