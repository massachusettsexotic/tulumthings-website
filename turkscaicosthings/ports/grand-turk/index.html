Grand Turk cruise port guide

Turks & Caicos Islands · from TurksCaicosThings, the offline iPhone guide

Where ships dock

Grand Turk Cruise Center pier

Ships dock at the Grand Turk Cruise Center at the south-west end of the island; the beach and shops are right at the pier. Cockburn Town is about 4 km (2½ miles) north by taxi or scooter. Providenciales is a separate island, reached by plane, not from the ship.

Typically about 8 AM – 5 PM · check today's all-aboard in your ship's daily program

All-aboard is in ship time. Check the daily programme and be back at the gangway 30 minutes early.

Top places near the pier

Walking times come from the app: straight-line distance plus 30% for streets, at an easy 4.5 km/h. Places further than an hour on foot show the distance instead.

  1. Governor's Beach

    27 min walk · Beaches

    Grand Turk's best beach, south of town beside the Governor's residence — casuarina shade, calm water, and the famous wall just offshore where the sea floor drops from 10 metres to 2,000.

  2. Gibbs Cay

    3 mi / 5 km from the pier — taxi or shuttle · Boat Trips & Fishing

    A sandbar cay off Grand Turk where the southern stingrays swim up to you in knee-deep water — the excursion every cruise passenger remembers, and better on a small boat from town.

  3. Cockburn Town

    4 mi / 6 km from the pier — taxi or shuttle · Landmarks & Nature

    The capital of the country, and a village: Bermudian-style houses with wooden verandas on Front Street, salt-pond ruins, wandering donkeys, and a pace that stopped in about 1950.

  4. Turks and Caicos National Museum

    4 mi / 6 km from the pier — taxi or shuttle · Museums & Culture

    In the 180-year-old Guinep House on Front Street: the Molasses Reef wreck (the oldest European shipwreck excavated in the Americas), Lucayan artefacts, the salt industry, and John Glenn's 1962 splashdown off Grand Turk.

  5. Grand Turk Lighthouse

    8 mi / 12 km from the pier — taxi or shuttle · Landmarks & Nature

    The 1852 cast-iron lighthouse shipped from London to the island's northern tip, above cliffs and a blue-black ocean — whales pass below in winter, and the view runs the length of the island.

Getting around: what the app tells you

Read the bill before you tip
Almost every restaurant adds a 10–15% service charge and the 12% government tax on top of the menu price, and the card machine will still cheerfully offer a tip line. Look for the 'service' line: if it's there, the staff have been tipped; add a few dollars only for something special. If it isn't, 15–20%.
Snorkel from shore — and never stand on it
You don't need a boat: Bight Reef (Coral Gardens) and Smith's Reef are a short swim from the sand, with marked buoys, and are better than most paid trips. Go before 10 AM for calm water, follow the ropes, and float — a fin on the coral kills it, and a coral cut gets infected. Mineral sunscreen only; the reef is a protected park, and rangers do check.
Rent the car, drive on the left, mind the roundabouts
Two taxi rides cost more than a day's rental, so most visitors drive. Traffic keeps left; the rental will likely be a left-hand-drive car anyway. At roundabouts you go clockwise and yield to traffic already circling from your right — this is where every visitor's near-miss happens. Roads are unlit and potholed off the main highway, so drive by daylight to Blue Hills and the south shore. Police stop for phones and seatbelts; the limit is 40 mph (64 km/h) on Leeward Highway and 20 in Grace Bay.
Do the outer islands right: early ferry, cash, car at the dock
The North Caicos ferry leaves Walkin Marina at Leeward; take the first sailing (around 7:30–8 AM), because the last one back is mid-afternoon and there's no evening boat. Reserve a rental car for pickup at the Sandy Point dock — without one you'll see nothing — and bring cash: no ATMs, and the plantation and cave guides don't take cards. Middle Caicos is over a causeway, 45 minutes further; Mudjin Harbour first, caves at midday, back to the dock with an hour to spare. Grand Turk is a separate day: fly, don't sail.
