Crown Bay cruise port guide

St. Thomas, US Virgin Islands · from StThomasThings, the offline iPhone guide

Where ships dock

Crown Bay cruise dock

Crown Bay is the second pier, 3 km west of downtown by the old submarine base. Take a safari taxi into town; Frenchtown and the Water Island ferry are a short walk. The road out of Crown Bay jams when several ships leave at once.

Typically 8 AM – 5 PM · island clocks are Atlantic Standard Time all year — check whether your ship keeps a different time

All-aboard is in ship time. Check the daily programme and be back at the gangway 30 minutes early.

Top places near the pier

Walking times come from the app: straight-line distance plus 30% for streets, at an easy 4.5 km/h. Places further than an hour on foot show the distance instead.

  1. Crown Bay Marina

    6 min walk · Day Sails & Charters

    The marina beside the Crown Bay cruise pier, with a few bars and restaurants on the docks and the Water Island ferry landing.

  2. Lindbergh Bay Beach

    17 min walk · Beaches

    A calm, sandy beach right beside the airport runway, with hotels and a beach bar — the easy swim for Crown Bay passengers.

  3. Frenchtown

    24 min walk · Forts & Landmarks

    A tiny fishing village on the harbour west of downtown, settled by families from St. Barthélemy in the 1800s — the fishing boats still come in here, and the lanes have some of the island's best restaurants.

  4. Water Island

    27 min walk · Parks & Reserves

    The fourth US Virgin Island — about 500 acres, a few hundred residents, no big hotels — just across the harbour from Crown Bay, with a sleepy beach day on the other side.

  5. Honeymoon Beach (Water Island)

    29 min walk · Beaches

    A calm, sandy beach on Water Island, a 10-minute ferry ride and a short walk from Crown Bay — a beach bar, rentals and water that's usually flat.

  6. Petite Pump Room

    33 min walk · Restaurants

    A long-running local restaurant above the ferry terminal on the waterfront, serving West Indian breakfast and lunch — johnnycakes, fish, stewed conch — with a harbour view.

  7. Cathedral of St. Peter and St. Paul

    39 min walk · Forts & Landmarks

    The Roman Catholic cathedral of Charlotte Amalie, built in the 1840s, with murals inside painted by Belgian artists in the 1890s.

  8. Hassel Island

    41 min walk · Parks & Reserves

    The island that guards Charlotte Amalie harbour, part of Virgin Islands National Park, with 19th-century forts, a ruined marine railway and short trails.

  9. Market Square

    41 min walk · Markets & Craft

    Rothschild Francis Market Square at the west end of Main Street, the old Danish marketplace under a cast-iron roof — fruit, vegetables and local produce on Saturday mornings.

  10. Charlotte Amalie Waterfront

    43 min walk · Forts & Landmarks

    Veterans Drive and the harbour promenade from Fort Christian to the ferry terminal: seaplanes, ferries, day-sail boats and the pastel town climbing the hills behind.

  11. Palm Passage

    43 min walk · Shopping & Souvenirs

    A shaded arcade of duty-free shops in the old warehouses between Main Street and the waterfront.

  12. Royal Dane Mall

    43 min walk · Shopping & Souvenirs

    The best of the old warehouse alleys between Main Street and the waterfront: brick arches, shade, small shops and cafés — and Gladys' Cafe.

  13. Gladys' Cafe

    44 min walk · Restaurants

    A Charlotte Amalie institution in the Royal Dane Mall: West Indian breakfast and lunch — saltfish, conch in butter sauce, callaloo — and a wall of her own hot sauces for sale.

  14. Main Street

    45 min walk · Shopping & Souvenirs

    Dronningens Gade, the old Danish high street, lined with 18th-century stone warehouses now full of duty-free jewellery, watches, liquor and perfume, with narrow alleys to the waterfront between them.

  15. Brewers Bay Beach

    46 min walk · Beaches

    A long, quiet beach beside the University of the Virgin Islands, locals' favourite, with shallow sea grass where turtles feed.

Getting around: what the app tells you

Ask the fare, and count heads
Taxi fares are per person and per destination, set by the VI Taxicab Commission, and every taxi should carry the rate sheet. Say the destination, ask 'how much per person?', and agree before you sit down. Share a safari with other passengers going the same way and it's the cheapest ride on the island.
St. John: go early, come back early
The best day on St. John starts on the first ferry you can reach from Red Hook. Coming back, allow the 20-minute crossing, a wait for the next boat, and 30–40 minutes of taxi to the pier — so catch a ferry at least two hours before all-aboard.
Set an alarm on ship time
Island clocks are Atlantic Standard Time, all year. Ships often keep a different time. Before you go ashore, check the time on the ship's app or a clock on board and set your phone alarm by that — the ship will not wait.
